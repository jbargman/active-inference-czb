## 3 The convention gap, quantified

The fixture scripts each onset on `theta_dot = W*dv / max(dx, 0.5)^2` with `dx` the raw longitudinal separation. The analysis path computes `theta_dot = W*dv / (gap^2 + W^2/4)` with `gap` EDGE-TO-EDGE, as every video card does. Three differences separate them, and the third is the large one.

| difference | effect on theta_dot |
|---|---|
| edge-to-edge vs raw separation (11.04 m vs 15.54 m at one onset) | x1.98 |
| exact derivative vs small-angle (the `+ W^2/4` term) | x0.993 |
| the fixture's scripted 0.2 s reaction delay, during which the gap keeps closing | included in both columns of section 1 |

So the dominant term is the **gap convention**, worth a factor of about 2.0 at this onset, while the exact-derivative correction is under a percent. A level fitted on one convention and applied under the other is wrong by that factor. This is the single most important thing to fix with VCC before their adapter is written.

## 4 What the interface schema is missing

Found by writing the loader against it. Each is a change to `transfer/interface_schema.yaml`, and each is cheap now and expensive after VCC has built an adapter.

1. **One `ref_point` column cannot serve both vehicles (NDS.Q2).** The schema carries a single `ref_point` for an event, which assumes the ego and the partner are referenced the same way. In naturalistic data they essentially never are: the ego position comes from the vehicle's own signals (rear axle or centre of gravity) and the partner's from radar or camera (typically the nearest reflecting surface). The schema needs `ego_ref_point` and `oth_ref_point` separately.
2. **`rear_axle` is not resolvable to a bumper position (NDS.Q2).** Converting one needs the front overhang; the schema carries only total length. Either add `ego_front_overhang` / `oth_front_overhang`, or state that positions are delivered at the vehicle centre. The loader raises rather than guessing.
3. **The lateral frame of `ego_y` and `oth_y` is ambiguous (NDS.Q3).** The schema calls `ego_y` a 'signed lateral offset from lane center (preferred)' and `oth_y` a 'partner lateral position'. The gate needs their DIFFERENCE, so both must be in one frame. If the ego's is lane-relative and the partner's is absolute, `l0` is wrong by the ego's own lane offset, which is exactly the quantity that varies during a cut-in.
4. **`t_brake_onset` has no stated criterion (NDS.Q4).** The axis value at the onset is the observation, so the onset definition sets the level. A pedal-switch criterion and a deceleration-threshold criterion differ by a few tenths of a second, and the gap closes throughout. The schema should carry the criterion as a field, or fix one in the protocol.
5. **`valid` is not required to hold at the onset (NDS.Q5).** The partner track may be interpolated exactly where the observation is read. The loader reports `valid_at_onset`; the analysis should exclude or flag those events, and the schema should say so.

A sixth point is a caution rather than a gap: `theta_dot` scales linearly with the partner's width, so a class-default width propagates straight into the axis. `geometry_default` flags it (0 of 12 events here), and the analysis should report what share of its events carry defaulted geometry.
