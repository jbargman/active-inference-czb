## 4 Which factor holds the inversion, and which factor could carry the axis

The six factors' own contributions to eps at the released defaults. ## 5 The verdict on the decision rule

**The rule as pre-stated fires (ii): some vector gives rho(log eps, gap) < 0.** 306 of 648 do. But the rule was written as a binary on a sign, and the result is not binary, so what it fires on has to be said exactly.

**The sign is reachable inside the family; the ordering is not.** The best vector reaches rho_gap -0.267 against the participants' own -0.862, and **0 of 648 vectors** order more than half the 24 matched-TTC rows the way the data do -- the best manages 7 of 24. The best held-out score is 0.2705, against the gated looming rule's 0.1027 + 0.01 and chance 0.320. So no driver in this family has a comfort zone that orders these cells.

**What does most of the work is the severity's linearity in closing speed.** Section 2: flattening it moves the median rho_gap from +0.296 to -0.249. Every one of the top vectors has a flat severity and the shortest preferred TTC in the grid (tau_inv_mu 0.40, TTC 2.5 s). That is a statement about the FORM -- the collision cost's proportionality to impact speed -- and not about a constant a driver could differ in.

**The reading, which is the same shape as card JJ.2b's.** Changing the framing (JJ.2b: the model's own policy space) or the form (here: a severity that does not grow with speed) repairs the DIRECTION of the ordering and leaves the MAGNITUDE at chance. Two independent lines now say that, and section 4 says why: on these stimuli eps is the collision factor plus the safety factor and nothing else, and the two point in opposite directions with respect to the response (rho with the share +0.358 and -0.861). A sum of one factor that orders weakly and one that orders backwards cannot be a comfort-zone scalar at any parameter vector.
