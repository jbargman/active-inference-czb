Two readings follow from this table and are stated as readings, not decisions.

1. **Delta G is a value-of-action quantity, and it collapses where no action helps.** It is large where braking would avert a collision that continuing would cause, and small both where nothing is going to happen and where the gap is already too small for any policy in the menu to avoid contact: there the best alternative's G rises with G(continue) and the difference closes. The menu's own cost is the other half of the mechanism -- braking at -3 m/s^2 costs a fixed 20308 nats of control effort over the horizon whatever the scene is doing (sigma_a = 0.1 m/s^2 in the released preference), so the minimum is floored.

2. **The released magnitude still grades with speed rather than with the gap.** That is the R.2 pipeline review's finding (`docs/r2_pipeline_review.md` section 3) reappearing inside the rollouts, and it is what rule (c) counts: the axis is anti-ordered within matched-TTC rows, where the design varies the gap and the speed together.

A level on log G(continue) alone -- the rollout's own criticality without the policy comparison, which is NOT this card's axis -- scores 0.3202 held out post-onset and 0.5381 at the pre-onset cells, against the gated looming rule's 0.1027 and 0.0319. It is reported so that the policy comparison and the preference function are not blamed for one another.
