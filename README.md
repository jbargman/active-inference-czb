# Waymo active inference — papers, replication, surprise metrics, comfort-zone boundaries

Working repository for the active-inference driver-modeling line of work: reading the Waymo /
TU-Delft papers, replicating the collision-avoidance model, building a reusable library of
surprise measures, and developing a method for establishing driver **comfort-zone boundaries**.

## What works, and how well

**Surprise library** (`src/surprise/`) — solid. Implements the three families from
Modirshanechi et al.'s taxonomy plus the two measures introduced by Dinparastdjadid et al.:

- probabilistic mismatch: surprisal, **residual information**, Macedo's S8, Bayes-factor
  surprise, state prediction error
- belief mismatch: Bayesian surprise, **antithesis**, postdictive, confidence-corrected
- observation mismatch: absolute / squared error, unsigned RPE
- preference-relative: pragmatic value, residual information of pragmatic value, and the
  evidence accumulator that turns it into response timing

Works on Gaussians, Gaussian mixtures (trajectory-predictor output), particle sets
(active-inference beliefs) and categorical distributions through a common interface. The
tests verify the *claims*, not just that the code runs: the zero-floor property, invariance to
discretization, the Modirshanechi equivalences (`S_SPE = 1 − exp(−S_Sh)`), and that antithesis
silences mode-narrowing and mode-removal while firing on genuine surprise.

**Replication of the authors' model** (`external/aica` + `replication/`) — runs; two conditions
tested. At the paper's **Fig. 3b** case (v0 = 25 m/s, 1.0 s gap) it **reproduces the published
result**: response time 0.80 s against ~0.6–0.8 s, with the correct brake-and-swerve maneuver.
At **Fig. 3a** (v0 = 15 m/s, 1.5 s gap) it gives 0.92 s against 1.4 s and swerves where the
paper brakes only. Responding early and swerving at both conditions — correct at one, wrong at
the other — is what an over-pessimistic `a_tar_min` predicts, and the cause is identified: the
calibration table shipped with the code does not span the paper's operating range, so that
parameter saturates at its most pessimistic value. Cost: ~18 s of CPU per simulated step.

**Independent re-implementation** (`src/aidriver/`) — mechanisms and preference function
faithful (verified against the Supplementary Information). Run over the paper's full
28-condition sweep (140 runs), it **reproduces 2 of the 6 published relations**: maneuver
choice versus speed, and braking magnitude versus urgency — the two that rest on the
preference function. The four that fail all depend on evidence-accumulation timing, where
response times are wildly dispersed (median 0.20 s, sd 1.23 s) and the collision rate is 34%
against the paper's "only at the shortest time gap". Do not use it for response-timing claims
yet. `notes/05_validation.md` has the comparison; `notes/03_replication.md` the diagnosis.

**Comfort-zone method** (`src/comfortzone/`) — this is the part built to be used. It depends
only on the verified pieces (the preference function and residual information), not on the
closed-loop controller. Gives:

- a scalar comfort-zone field over any 2-D slice of the state space, decomposable into the six
  preference terms so exceedances can be attributed to a cause;
- a **closed-form** comfort-zone boundary for car following, cross-checked against the numeric
  level set to 0.000 m;
- comfort vs dread boundaries as a single interpretable choice of allowed deceleration;
- "extra motives" as parameter changes with quantified effects;
- calibration of the boundary level against observed response onsets, from recorded kinematics
  alone — no model roll-out required, which is what makes it applicable to naturalistic data.

Sample output (steady following, lead assumed capable of −6 m/s², 1 s response time):

| speed | THW* at 8 m/s² (dread) | at 4 m/s² (comfort) |
|---|---|---|
| 10 m/s | 0.85 s | 1.48 s |
| 15 m/s | 0.73 s | 1.67 s |
| 20 m/s | 0.61 s | 1.86 s |
| 30 m/s | 0.40 s | 2.27 s |

## Important caveat

No human data has been used anywhere in this repository. Every number is a property of the
model. The comfort-zone method is a construction, not yet a finding — §7 of
`notes/04_comfort_zone_method.md` sets out the cross-scenario generalization test that would
turn it into one.
