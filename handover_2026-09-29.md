## 5 What this session may have got wrong

- **The direction of the display effect is unresolved.** DT.1's corrected numbers assume the
  participants judged the looming at their eye; DT.2 says the left-turn participants did not. My
  recommendation flipped between the two cards (DT1.Q2); both versions are to be reported until
  DT2.Q1 decides.
- **DT.2 maps the track onto the video's stimulus geometry.** The 2013 protocol has no
  decision-moment kinematics. A different decision moment on the track shifts distances, but I argued
  it cannot bridge a factor of 2.36; that argument is mine, not a result.
- **"Human looming thresholds are of the same order as 0.00215 rad/s"** (explanation document §6.5)
  is from memory and marked "to be checked". The threshold quoted is the released model's.
- **The horizon-sum mechanism has two parts, and I nearly dropped one.** Across cells the sum counts
  the steps before contact: the review's check found +0.920 with the TTC for the braking-margin sum.
  Within rows of equal TTC it is dominated by its value at contact, which scales with the closing
  speed (JJ.12). While writing chapter 18 I first replaced the first reading with the second; the
  chapter now gives both. `docs/looming_as_free_energy.md` and `docs/note_for_jj_horizon_sum.md` give
  only the first, which is right across cells but does not explain the within-row ordering. Add
  JJ.12's sentence before either is sent.
- **The GZ.1 and GZ.2 numbers in the authors' new version** (3.2 s, 4.6 s, ×100 noise) were taken from
  the worklog, not re-derived in this session. Their outputs are in `replication/causation/gz1/` and
  `gz2/`.
- **The NM.3 onset definition was changed twice before any result** (dated in its docstring); the
  level comparison with the model is weak for the reasons stated there.
- **Worklog formatting:** until today's collector fix, two queries (DT2.Q1, NC3C.Q1) absorbed the
  paragraphs that followed them in the register. The worklog itself was always right.
- **The prompt count** excludes 57 task notifications that the log records as prompts; prompts typed
  on another machine would be missing (none were found).
