## 1 The review: what was checked and what it found

The previous arc's own §9 flagged two things to verify; both were run, and the review
went beyond them. Findings, in decreasing order of consequence:

**1.1 The B-versus-C "statistically solid" claim was overstated, and is now corrected.**
The paired-difference analysis behind results doc §4.3b-ii (θ_C − θ_B = 0.061, 95% HDI
[0.037, 0.107], "same sign on every resample") had no committed script. Reproducing it
revealed it had resampled the reference while holding **both synthetic sides fixed**,
omitting the synthetic-side sampling variance — which is independent between conditions
and does not cancel in the difference. The committed reproduction
(`replication/causation/paired_difference.py`, output
`out/summary_paired_difference_fullp_abn.md`) runs all three schemes: ref-only
reproduces the old numbers; under the project's own population convention the result is
mean 0.055, 95% HDI [−0.006, 0.110], **P(θ_C > θ_B) ≈ 0.97**. The ordering is strongly
supported — consistent in point estimates at every bin count, corroborated by the
braking aggregate (B within 0.3%, C over-braking 35%), ~0.97 posterior probability —
but not "every resample". Corrected in results doc §4.3b-ii and §8, handbook ch. 08,
README, and a bracketed correction in `handover_2026-08-26.md` §3.6. The irony that the
arc devoted to uncertainty accounting produced one more incomplete uncertainty
accounting is noted here so a successor keeps checking.

**1.2 The stored bin-sensitivity summary and the results doc quoted intervals from
different resampling conventions, without saying so.** The stored sweep predated the
population default; the doc's N = 20 remark quoted a third, intermediate run whose HDI
matched neither. The sweep is regenerated under the population convention (its header
now states the convention), and the θ-bias remark in §4.4 is corrected: under cases
resampling B's N = 20 HDI [0.347, 0.773] excludes the point estimate 0.275; under the
population convention it is [0.263, 0.389] and does not.

**1.3 The §4.3 hand-copied intervals check out.** Every θ, Θ, HDI and aggregate cell in
§4.3/4.3b/4.3c matches `summary_fullp_abn.md` exactly.

**1.4 Code defects found and fixed** (none affected published numbers, verified):

## 2 The continuous lane-entry form (settled) and the staging fix

The full argument is `docs/lane_entry_note.md`; handbook ch. 04's open-problem section
now records the resolution ({{R4}}, dark purple). In one paragraph: the binary lane
gates are replaced by the **lateral overlap fraction predicted at the moment of
longitudinal closure** (the lateral offset projected forward at its closing rate over
the longitudinal TTC, clamped at centering — Jonas's τ_lat-versus-TTC comparison
expressed as an overlap), and the saturated safety magnitude by the **counterfactual
residual impact speed** √max(0, v_react² − 2 a_max d_avail), which is zero exactly at
the model's own boundary a_req = −a_max and turns the cliff into a ramp. Both changes
are parameter-free; both reduce exactly to the released forms in every geometry the
released scenarios sustain; both are flag-gated in `src/aidriver/preferences.py`
(`lane_entry_continuous`, `counterfactual_residual_severity`, defaults off) so every
published number is untouched. The note argues the reading on which this is the
closed-form of the expectation the closed loop already computes by rollout, not a new
preference. The desired speed is staged from the clip in `cutin_params` — the released
scenarios stage `v_ego_des` the same way.

Validation (`replication/czb/cutin_field_check.py`, `figures/cutin_field_check.png`):
pre-onset deficit exactly zero in every clip; deficit ordered by criticality at every
truncation point C2–C6 for cars and trucks; ramps, not steps, within the response
window. 19 new property tests (`tests/test_cutin.py`) including the released-limit
identities and a continuity test that caught a genuinely discontinuous first design
(recorded in the note as a discarded intermediate).

Two empirical findings worth carrying:

- **The mild truck cut-ins (TTC6–8) have zero dread-field deficit through most of the
  response window** — at those gaps the counterfactual crash is avoidable at
  a_max = 8, so the field is silent where humans plausibly still press. The
  comfort–dread distinction (allowed deceleration below capability) is therefore a
  necessity for the fitting, not a refinement.
- **Onset detection was 1–2 frames late** (2%-of-span threshold), which put genuinely
  moving frames on the pre-onset side of the C1 anchor; fixed to an absolute 0.03 m
  threshold. Found via the C1 covariate check, which is the kind of thing only the
  data pipeline surfaces.

## 3 Fitting preparation (done) and the methods answer

`docs/czb_fitting_plan.md` is the document; `src/comfortzone/czb_data.py` the pipeline;
`replication/czb/pilot_surface_fit.py` the committed stage-0 fit.

The answer to "do we have optimal methods with so many parameters": the fear is
misplaced, by design. Everything upstream of the boundary is frozen (released
parameters; parameter-free structural changes; staging from the clip), the stimulus
field is precomputed once per clip, and all 3 096 Random cut-in trials share 18
covariate values. What remains is a generalized linear mixed model — ~5–8 population
parameters plus shrunk per-driver random effects — for which MAP + Laplace in
CPU-torch is entirely adequate, with a synthetic-recovery harness as the first step.
No simulator in the likelihood, no CMA-ES, no ABC.

Stage-0 feasibility, already run: a two-parameter probit on the running-max deficit
reproduces the study's 18-cell response surface with **correlation 0.90, RMSE 0.125**
— and its residual (too flat in time within a criticality level) is precisely the
accumulator's signature, motivating stage 2 before it is fitted. The Button pipeline
works too: 2 396 trials, press times on the time-since-onset axis, medians ordered by
criticality (TTC4 0.36 s → TTC8 1.8 s), only 0.5% never press.

## 6 Caveats on the day sections

Written by the session that did the work. The stage-0 fit is population-level and
deliberately naive; do not quote its c or σ as boundary estimates. The C1 covariate
cells are small but nonzero for TTC6/8 (the anticipation weight activates on the first
frame of motion); the design's bias term absorbs this, but anyone comparing C1
predictions to the "pure baseline" reading should know it is not exactly pure. The
Button-side covariates at the press assume the clip's field applies up to the press
frame; the 23 overshoot trials are dropped by the loader, per the dataset's own
guidance.

## 7 The evening: assessment and the discussion that amended the plan

Jonas asked for an honest assessment of active inference for CZB quantification, then
pushed on it in two rounds. Everything below is committed; the documents carry the full
arguments.

**The assessment** (`docs/active_inference_for_czb_assessment.md`): the method uses a
thin slice of the framework (preference field + accumulator; planning and epistemic
value off); the distinctive value lives in the untested transfer claim and the dual
role (one scalar defines the boundary *and* predicts response timing); seven risks with
severities; for ADAS triggering the percentile choice dominates and comfort is an
acceptance criterion. Verdict: pursue, transfer test as the gate, describe more
modestly than the "active inference" label suggests.

**Numbers produced during the discussion** (all committed under `replication/czb/`):

- *Baselines and cross-validation* (`pilot_surface_fit.py`): the field beats every
  single kinematic scalar at matched parameter count but loses within-scenario to
  richer regressions, and the result is NOT overfitting — held-out
  (leave-one-criticality-out) the field gives corr 0.864 against **Jonas's 2D state
  rule** (probit on 1/TTC + lateral offset) at **0.921**, now the named comparator.
  The 2D rule's second observable is scenario-specific, so the transfer test is a
  three-way race: one-scalar field vs per-scenario 2D rules vs the elliptical joint.
- *Perceived safety* (`ps_vs_field.py`, `figures/ps_vs_field.png`): the PS column is
  perceived **unsafety** (dictionary gotcha 1). Against the field: cell-level
  Spearman ρ = 0.93, trial-level 0.58, within-criticality 0.39–0.64. The ratings
  keep rising where the static field compresses — the accumulator's signature a
  third time.
- *Epistemic value, from the authors' own ablations*
  (`epistemic_ablation_check.py`): the deposit contains α = 0 runs. Rear-end, 28
  matched configurations: collision difference exactly 0.000; intersection within
  0.005; oncoming 5.7 points *fewer* collisions with the term on (3 configurations).
  Inert longitudinally — our α = 0 reproduces the authors' own ablation — possibly
  useful laterally.
- *Boundary sensitivity*: relative speed is in the closed form (comfort boundary at
  20 m/s: 1.86 s steady → 2.59 s closing at 5 m/s); the 1.86 stacks three worst-case
  conventions (a_OV,min = −4 alone gives 1.03 s).
- *Percentile-versus-absolute* (`figures/percentile_vs_absolute.png`): shared
  measurement offsets preserve who sits at a percentile but shift when a trigger
  placed there fires.

**Plan amendments Jonas made** (folded into `docs/czb_validation_roadmap.md` §0b, 4b,
4c and `docs/czb_fitting_plan.md`): the comfort **and** dread levels are both fitted —
the DZB is the deceleration drivers do not voluntarily exceed, a behavioral limit, not
the physical a_max, so 4/8 are staging anchors only; a secondary model fits a_OV,min
and t_react at population level (identifiable from cross-speed variation; primary
keeps them fixed for falsifiability); planning and epistemic value are reconsidered on
evidence, not dropped (stage E: E.1 closed-form vs sampled expectation, E.2 an
uncertainty term tested on truck-vs-car cut-ins, E.3 a policy-set field for the
transfer scenarios); NDS access is for measuring the paradigm offset, exposure
denominators, a percentile consistency check, and instruction-free transfer events;
`CZB_2` is treated as the human decision pending a new dataset; motor latency fixed at
0.25 s (bracket 0.15/0.35, citation flagged unverified); the Sequence design stays
excluded; the deliverable is the hierarchical **population distribution** (deployment
is group-level; per-driver effects are instruments for estimating the spread).
