# Handover: the 2026-08-28 arc — the transfer scenario, the shape decision, and a scoping result

*Written 2026-08-28. Read `handover.md` first for standing context, then
`handover_2026-08-26.md` and `handover_2026-08-27.md` (whose §12 records review gate
R.1). This file covers only this arc. It is written by the session that did the work and

## 0 What this arc was

It began as card B.1 — build the cyclist-overtake field — and turned into a scoping
result about what the study-1 data can and cannot establish. Nothing that was previously
computed has been found *wrong*; what changed is what those computations can be claimed
to show. Two datasets arrived mid-arc (`02_Cut-in`, `03_CAMP`) and the first of them
answered a question that had been referred to a review gate.

## 1 The finding that reframes the rest

**In the study-1 cut-in design, relative speed is constant at 2.78 m/s across every one
of the 18 cells, so correlation(gap, TTC) = 1.0000.** Time headway and required
deceleration are fixed functions of the same single number. The longitudinal dimension of
that design has one degree of freedom.

Everything longitudinal fitted on study 1 — the stage-0 correlation of 0.90, the stage-1
boundary level of 5 352, the population percentile — is therefore equally consistent with
a driver who thresholds the preference field and a driver who thresholds **the gap**. The
fits are not wrong and the boundary is well estimated on its own scale. But they cannot
be cited as evidence that the field's kinematic content is the right description, because
the design contains no contrast that separates it from the simplest possible alternative.
Reproduced by `replication/czb/cutin2_scope.py` §0.

This should have been noticed earlier. It was not, and the practical cost is that several
sessions of work were spent refining an estimator on a design that cannot discriminate
the model it estimates.

## 2 The second cut-in study breaks the collinearity, and rules against us

`02_Cut-in`: 10 944 trials, 168 participants, the same three response variables as study
1 including the ordered braking question. Its delta-velocity factor is crossed with TTC
and the two multiply into distance by construction, so matched-TTC cells span gaps from
1.6 m to 78 m — six-fold, in the scenario whose field already exists.

- **All 24 matched-TTC rows run negative.** At identical time-to-collision, a larger gap
  means less intervention, by as much as 0.042 against 0.818 at TTC 4.7 s. Time alone
  does not determine the response.
- Over all cells: **gap orders the response at ρ = −0.887**, time-to-collision at
  **−0.807**, and required deceleration at **+0.238**. The last has the sign a
  demand-based model wants but a negligible magnitude — so the quantity our safety terms
  are built from barely orders these cells while gap orders them almost perfectly.
- It cannot separate gap from closing speed or headway, which are perfectly confounded
  at matched TTC by construction. It shows a time measure is insufficient without saying
  which ingredient is missing.

`03_CAMP` is described only (19 files); no use proposed, per Jonas.

## 3 Card B.1: the field does not represent the cyclist overtake

`src/comfortzone/overtake.py`, `docs/overtake_construction_note.md`. The loader is
validated against the study's own labels (edge clearance at the pass 0.506 / 1.004 /
1.501 m against 0.5 / 1 / 1.5). Three corrections to the card: roles must come from the
instructed vehicle, because the cut-in's lateral-span rule is **inverted** here (the ego
moves 2.24 m, the cyclist 0.16); the lateral coordinate is `Location_Y`, since `Offset`
inverts the criticality ordering; and the cyclist's width is in the trace (0.582 m).

The card's premise fails. The field orders the 15 cells at Spearman **+0.402** while the
clearance label alone orders them at **−0.833**, because `p_lane` sits at 0.843–1.000
through the entire response window and cannot see the manipulated variable. The
scenario's criticality is a lateral **comfort margin** — 0.5 m is uncomfortable while
being uncontroversially collision-free — and the preference function's lateral machinery
is collision-oriented. A symmetric-projection repair (`lane_entry_bidirectional`) was
built, tested, and **over-corrects**: `p_lane` collapses to 0 by C3 and the cells end up
ordered −0.537. Kept, default off, as tested-and-rejected.

**But the transfer itself works once the baseline is matched.** Frozen transfer scores
RMSE 0.176 against chance 0.158 — worse than chance — while freeing **only the
per-scenario lapse** gives 0.129, past chance and within 0.016 of the full-refit ceiling
0.113. The pre-onset rate is 0.081 in the cut-in and 0.198 in the overtake, so a no-shift
primary is mis-specified at the baseline before the boundary is consulted. The lapse is
identified by C1 cells alone. Roadmap §2 step 4 and card B.4 amended accordingly.

## 4 The one-scalar claim has independent support

`replication/czb/cross_scenario_consistency.py`, and this needs no field at all. All 43
participants saw all four Random scenarios, so the claim's central prediction — that a
driver carries one level — is testable directly. Per-driver criticality-adjusted
propensity correlates **+0.50 to +0.74** across the six scenario pairs, which is
**0.53–0.78 of the split-half reliability ceiling** (reliabilities 0.93–0.98), mean
**0.69**.

About 69% of the reliable per-driver signal is shared across scenarios. That is the
strongest evidence for the CZB framing that does not depend on any field construction,
and it simultaneously caps stage B: a third of reliable per-driver variance is
scenario-specific, so B.4 should be scored against ~0.69 rather than against perfection.

## 5 The S-shaped lane-entry ramp, decided

Jonas's proposal, implemented as `lane_entry_shape(u, k)` — a normalized logistic with
g(0) = 0, g(1) = 1 exactly for every k and g(u; 0) = u, so the linear ramp is nested.
Swept on the cut-in surface with the stage-0 probit refitted at each k: RMSE 0.1189
linear, minimum **0.1154 at k ≈ 12**, reflected shape worse at 0.1211. Direction
supported, gain modest (2.9%). The k → ∞ limit is the released binary gate and is the
worst of the family (0.1457), which independently corroborates the 2026-08-27 continuity
decision.

**Decided by Jonas: k is fixed at 12**, applied on the CZB staging path only
(`comfortzone.cutin.CZB_LANE_ENTRY_SHAPE_K`); `PreferenceParams` still defaults to 0 so
causation work and released comparisons are untouched. Documented as a *calibrated
constant, not parameter-free* — chosen once against the cut-in response surface, same
standing as the released model's per-scenario a_OV,min — with the rule that it must never
become a per-scenario knob. **Consequence not to lose**: it changes the covariate in 3 of
18 cut-in cells (TTC4/C2 +26%, TTC6/C1 −27%, TTC8/C1 +8%), which are the lapse-identifying
cells, so stage 1 and the percentile table must be regenerated before being quoted (card
A.2.v2). **A first measurement of that consequence, seen in passing when the stage-2
refit re-fitted stage 1 under the new constant: the between-driver spread moves from
0.209 to 0.194**, about 7%. So every percentile quoted in this file and in `handover.md`
is a **pre-k=12 number** and will shift by roughly that much. They are kept as written
because they are what the committed outputs currently say; A.2.v2 replaces them.

## 6 R.1.Q2 and R.1.Q3, and a defect they exposed

The fitted lapse–threshold correlation was −0.727 under an independence assumption.
Simulating **independent** driver effects and refitting recovers −0.272, so 37% is
manufactured by the estimator's lapse/threshold trade-off; but the observed value is
2.9 sd below that arm and inside the arm simulated with a genuine −0.7, so some is real.
A.2.v2 must judge its fitted correlation against −0.27, not zero.

Two discriminating tests were then run. **The floor is a person trait**: per-driver C1
rate correlates +0.546 between cut-in and cyclist overtake, 61% of the reliability
ceiling. **Whether it is learned during the study is untestable on study 1**: the
within-session slope is +0.005 (SE 0.052), but a participant presses at C1 a median of
*once* in 24 trials, 17 of 43 drop out for having no variation and those are the low-floor
participants, and the smallest detectable slope is 0.146 against a real effect of +0.027.
That is not evidence of absence. The second cut-in study, which shows every block-1 clip
twice, gives the answer: P(intervene) rises from **0.547 to 0.575** between first and
second showing of an identical clip (3 456 pairs, SE 0.007). Jonas's anticipation account
is supported.

**And C1 is not a null scene.** At C1 the three cut-in conditions differ by a factor of
two in car-following state (gap 10.5 / 16.0 / 21.5 m, THW 0.34 / 0.52 / 0.70 s) and
behaviour is ordered accordingly (0.122 / 0.070 / 0.052) — an ordinary response to an
ordinary car-following situation, not anticipation. **The field ranks those cells
backwards**, assigning deficit 1 / 1509 / 2907. So the lapse is being fitted to cells
carrying real boundary signal with a covariate that ranks them wrongly: a fifth
explanation for R.1.Q2 that was on nobody's list, and the only one that is a defect in
our own construction. Blocker B2.Q1.

## 9 Caveats on this file

The transfer result in §3 rests on 15 cells with a narrow response range (0.14–0.69) and
one condition that is flat; RMSE differences of a few hundredths there are not decisive.
The 69% in §4 is a correlation of rate residuals, not of fitted boundary levels — it
supports the trait claim, it does not establish that the trait is *the comfort-zone
level*. And §1's collinearity finding is about the longitudinal dimension only; the
lateral manipulations in study 1 are genuinely independent of it.
