# Handover: the 2026-08-25 → 2026-08-26 arc

*Written 2026-08-26 for a successor session. `handover.md` remains the standing session
entry point and `HANDOFF.md` the deep research context; this file covers only what happened
in this arc, why, and where it landed. Read it after those two.*

## 0 What this arc was

It began as a request to explain the practical-equivalence ROPE used to score the
crash-causation study, and turned into three things: a substantial revision of how that
study's statistics are computed and reported, a diagnosis of why its metrics disagreed with
each other, and the opening of a new workstream on human data that arrived mid-arc.

Nothing about the study's **findings** was overturned. What changed is the machinery used to
express them, and in two places the machinery had been wrong.

## 2 Two defects found in our own code

Both were ours, not the method's. Both are fixed; both required regenerating outputs.

**2.1 The weighted bootstrap understated uncertainty by about a factor of two.**
`equivalence_test` drew reference *values* with probability proportional to weight and then
treated the resample as unweighted, which claims the precision of 5 000 independent draws.
The QUADRIS crash weights are concentrated enough (top 10% of scenarios carry 54% of the
weight) that the reference's effective sample size is **950**. Fixed by adding a `resample`
parameter with three schemes — `"population"` (default), `"cases"`, `"values"` (legacy) —
in `src/equivalence/test.py`. Point estimates were unaffected; every HDI published before
2026-08-26 was too narrow.

**2.2 The assessment used N = 5 bins where the method's own rule gives 20.**
`run_quadris.assess` hardcoded `n_bins=5`, correct when the reference was 100–200 seeds and
never revisited at full population. Quantified rather than simply fixed, because the sweep
showed N = 20 is unusable here: `replication/causation/bin_sensitivity.py`, results in
`docs/crash_causation_results.md` §4.4.

## 3 Findings

**3.1 The ROPE was unpassable, for reasons unrelated to the model.** A null calibration —
comparing the reference with itself — showed the original configuration could not be passed
by a model that is exactly right. Three causes compounded: uniform weights applied a
tolerance anchored to a 2% injury-risk baseline that our seeds never approach; the bootstrap
defect above; and treating the reference as a sample. Settling decision 1 drops the noise
floor from 0.177 to about **0.021**, at which point the verdicts describe the model.

**3.2 Choosing the bin-weight anchor P0 *is* choosing θthd.** They are one degree of freedom,
not two, since P0 rescales every weight by a constant and θ is linear in ω. Only the weight
*shape* is independent, and on our data it moves θ by 7%. This is why decision 3 is coherent
with decision 2 rather than an independent choice. (`equivalence_rope_note.md` §4.1)

**3.3 θ and Θ do not bound the weighted aggregate.** They constrain only how mass is
allocated between bins; the outer bins are open-ended. Condition B's actual mean injury risk
is +12.6% while its binned approximation is −2.7%, the gap sitting almost entirely in the
top band, where B's crashes average 0.0213 against the reference's 0.01615. (§3.2)

**3.4 Severity and response timing are near-independent in this population.** Conditions B
and C differ 4.7-fold in crash rate yet their severity quartiles agree to 0.3 m/s; 71% of
impact-speed variance is between-seed. Response timing decides *how many* crashes happen;
the scenario and whether the driver brakes at all decide *how hard*. Consequence worth
carrying: **a driver model validated only on crash severity distributions is close to
unconstrained in its response timing.** (`docs/severity_vs_timing.md`)

**3.5 θ misreads three of the four metrics.** It assumes the reference can be cut into N
equal pieces. Severity is smooth, so θ = 0.148 is meaningful. `t_nr` lives on a 0.05 s
lattice with 25 distinct reference values. `a_f,min` carries a 48% atom at zero that
collapses two of five quantile edges onto the same point, producing θ = 1.058 mechanically.
On interpretable bins the non-braking share agrees to 4.6%, and on the weighted mean
condition B is within **0.3%** while the CBM control over-brakes by **35%** — a far sharper
separation than θ ever showed.

**3.6 The B-versus-C ordering is statistically solid.** With the corrected wider intervals
the marginal HDIs overlap, which would naively suggest no separation. As a paired difference
with a shared reference draw: θ_C − θ_B = 0.061, 95% HDI [0.037, 0.107], same sign on every
resample. (`crash_causation_results.md` §4.3b-ii)

**3.7 Where the study now stands.** Neither condition reaches practical equivalence at a
defensible tolerance — B misses at 10% on its interval's upper end, C on every reading. The
gain from this arc is that the miss is now a statement about the model rather than about the
instrument.

## 4 The new data, and the cut-in workstream

**What arrived.** `external/01_studies/` — a crowdsourced clip-rating and button-press study,
80 participants, four scenarios, 67 stimulus kinematic traces at 10 Hz. Described in the new
handbook appendix, chapter 15. Jonas's own CZB position paper arrived alongside at
`external/QUADRARUM/Bärgman_On_CZB_as_basis_for_ADAS_adaptation 20260501.docx`.

**Three data-quality findings not in its documentation**, all verified against the traces:
`Acceleration_mps2` is unsigned and its definition could not be established (do not use it;
differentiate `Speed_mps`); the first and last frames are simulator teardown artifacts (~8.5%
of rows); truck bounding boxes are broken (`Length_m = 0`).

**Why the dataset suits the method.** The ego vehicle never responds — it holds a rigorously
constant speed through the cut-in clips — so the comfort-zone field along a clip is a fixed
function of time, computable once per stimulus and reused for every participant. The
fixed-clip design samples the response-time **CDF** at six known truncation points; the
button design samples the **density**. One accumulator predicts both.

**A cross-paradigm check worth knowing about.** P(intervene | clip ends at T) should equal
P(press ≤ T). It holds within 0.06 at high criticality and shows a systematic excess of early
pressing elsewhere, up to +0.26 at intermediate criticality — matching Jonas's documented
concern about satisficing in the button paradigm, and now quantified.

**The cut-in scenario is started, not finished.** `src/comfortzone/cutin.py` loads and
conditions the traces, assigns vehicle roles geometrically, and computes candidate
predictors. Running it produced the arc's most useful negative result: **with released
parameters the model does not discriminate TTC4 from TTC8 at all.** Decomposing the six
preference terms shows why — the speed term sits at a constant −483 because the default
desired speed is 15 m/s against a 30.5 m/s stimulus (a staging error, fix by setting it from
the clip), and the safety term is a saturated binary step, identical at a 10 m and a 21 m
gap, because both cut-ins end under 0.75 s headway. Only the collision term discriminates,
and it is two orders of magnitude smaller.

**The open design question**, and the thing to settle before any fitting: lane entry must
become continuous. Proposed in handbook ch. 04 — lateral **overlap fraction** for the present
state, and for the predictive half the comparison of **time-to-lateral-overlap** against the
longitudinal TTC, since a collision requires lateral overlap to have begun by the time the
gap closes. This also explains why lane-change *duration* was not a useful predictor in the
QUADRARUM group's own analyses: duration is a property of the manoeuvre alone, whereas what
matters is the lateral closing rate relative to the longitudinal one.

## 5 The CZB framing that was agreed

Recorded because Jonas confirmed it is the intended direction, and because it is falsifiable.

The conventional route estimates a percentile of an *observable* and inherits a multivariate
problem — each scenario has its own observables, and combining marginal percentiles under a
conjunction rule silently tightens the criterion (Jonas's own document works this through and
proposes an elliptical joint-percentile fix). The active-inference route estimates a
per-driver **scalar** level on the preference field, which has already absorbed speed, gap,
closing rate and lateral position into one number, so the population distribution is
one-dimensional by construction.

**The cross-scenario transfer test is what falsifies this**: fit the level on one scenario,
predict the other three unchanged. **If transfer fails, the fallback is the elliptical
formulation** — tracked deliberately.

A conceptual convergence worth keeping: Jonas's document defines the CZB as the point where
"the satisficing condition is violated ... and behavioral regulation becomes necessary",
which is very close to what the preference function *is* — a specification of acceptable
outcomes rather than an optimum.

The fitted level absorbs response bias, paradigm offset and motor latency as well as the
boundary. The design contains contrasts for most of these (the pre-onset condition identifies
response bias; the counterbalanced framing contrast identifies the framing shift; the
clip-versus-button contrast identifies the satisficing offset), but **motor latency is not
identified at all** and the paradigm offset can never be a clean modality effect because
Random always preceded Button. Documented with the open design decisions in
`docs/handbook/11_path_to_czb.md`.

(§2.1) and the threshold change from Wu's inherited 0.10/0.05 to the derived 0.188/0.089.
Both are documented in `docs/equivalence_rope_note.md`; neither is visible as a separate
step in the history.
