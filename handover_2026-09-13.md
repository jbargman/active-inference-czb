## 2 The findings a successor must not re-derive

**On what starts a response (card HS.1, `replication/czb/out/hs1_situational_surprise.md`).**
- A fixed-horizon constant-velocity predictor only registers a change that leaves its band within one
  horizon: at most a·h²/2 after a constant acceleration. Card Q5.1's pre-stated spread could not see a
  lane change at all. Do not run Q5.1 as written.
- The second study's traces need a 0.3 s velocity window (one-sample velocity leaves up to 0.89 m of
  error on steady driving). The first study's 10 Hz traces jitter longitudinally by ~7% of each frame's
  advance against every clock in the files; their longitudinal floor is metres, so surprise there can
  only be lateral.
- Cut-in: a parameter-free gate at the surprise onset keeps all 90 pre-onset clips closed (0.036) but
  scores 0.145 post-onset against card G.1's 0.103. At its tightest (σ0 0.05 m) it equals the ungated
  rule (0.1137), still 0.011 behind G.1. World, joint and relative references agree on the cut-in.
- Accumulating looming from the onset (the non-leaky integral of θ̇, i.e. θ(T) − θ(t_on)) loses to a
  state threshold on θ̇ at every spread (0.196 against 0.144 at the primary).
- Cyclist overtake: surprise about the cyclist never registers; whole-situation surprise registers the
  ego's pull-out 0.7–1.1 s after it starts, latest for the closest pass, while 17–58% of raters already
  intervene. Left turn: nothing registers before the clips end, while 11–91% intervene.
- Reading on file, not ruled on (HS1.Q3): anticipated conflict, not surprise, starts the response.
  Proposed next card PC.1, a scenario-agnostic projected-conflict gate.

**On Farewell's levels (card PT.1, `out/pt1_farewell_thresholds.md`).** Fixed at 0.02 rad/s or
0.2 s⁻¹, the comfort-judgment fits get clearly worse (0.153 against 0.113; 0.224 against 0.168). Fitted
50% points: 0.0336 rad/s and 0.327 s⁻¹, 1.6–1.7 times Farewell's. Farewell's level sits near the quarter
point of the comfort curve (25% at 0.0123 rad/s and 0.188 s⁻¹). Every cell is far above the model's
detection threshold, so none of this speaks to the perception switch.

**On the cut-in norm (card PN.1, `out/pn1_cutin_norm.md`, `docs/cutin_norm_proposal.md`).** A positional
norm withdraws trust later for slower lane changes (+0.53/+0.73/+0.93 s for 2/3/4 s); as an onset gate
it fails (0.288). The proposed crossing norm keys straddling on lateral speed and keeps every shown
straddling moment normal. The older `cutin_norm_weight` uses future information; retire it (PN1.Q2).

**On the gaze system (card GZ.1, `replication/causation/gz1/`).**
- **The released configuration does not hold sustained car following.** With no event, it starts
  braking at 3.2 s (1.5 s headway) or 4.6 s (2.0 s headway), with gaze choice on or off and with the
  authors' own scripted lead; 1–3 of 4 repeats stop. With perception noise ×100 it follows steadily.
  The cause is open (GZ1.Q2). This matters for anything with a long benign run-in: naturalistic data,
  and crash-causation windows that open early.
- Switching gaze choice on is one line on the constructed agent. With realistic noise the model never
  looks away (it has no reason to); with released noise its glances come from braking runs and are not
  interpreted. A near-blind glance is chosen about as readily as a mild one, and every glance lasts one
  0.2 s step, because the code returns gaze to the road with probability 1.
- Fitting glances to SHRP2 is a model-design task first — a motive to look away, a noise-dependent cost,
  a non-instant look-back — then days of simulation (GZ1.Q3).

**On the perception switch (a reading, no card).** The released model's 0.00215 rad/s is a detection
threshold; Farewell's 0.2 s⁻¹ (≈0.02 rad/s) is a response level ten times higher. A graded switch is
plausible at the detection level; a Farewell-like transition belongs on the accumulation gain or in
the preference, not in perception. Put to Julian as a question, not a claim.

## 5 What this session may have got wrong

- **Two post-run corrections in one night.** HS.1's first-study tests applied second-study settings to
  data with a different jitter floor, and PN.1's first minimum included frames nobody saw. Both are
  recorded in the scripts' docstrings with the first numbers. The pattern — measuring the noise floor
  per data source before choosing a spread, and restricting every statistic to the shown window — is
  worth a standing rule.
- **The GZ.1 scenario, and a withdrawn explanation.** In part a the released model braked by itself
  during what was meant to be steady following. I first blamed a headway beyond the calibration table
  and called 1.5 s "the authors' own benign-following value"; both were wrong (their benign script
  stages an oncoming pass, and part b's car stopped at 1.5 s too). An adapter check with the authors'
  own scripted lead decides whether the braking belongs to the model or to the replay class.
- **Spread values are judgments.** σ0 = 0.1 m and the crossing norm's 0.5 m/s are stated as unverified
  (HS1.Q1, PN1.Q1).
- **"Anticipation, not surprise" is an interpretation** of three scenarios on frozen video with the
  simplest predictor. A reference that knows the ego's intended path might register surprise earlier.
- **The handout now carries one-night-old results.** They are marked preliminary; Jonas should decide
  whether they go on paper.
