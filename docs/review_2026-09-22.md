## 1 The short version

The arc's numbers mostly reproduce. Several of its **conclusions do not follow from them**, and the
one that the handover calls the through-line is among those.

1. **"The released braking-margin term is inverted against the human response (rho = -0.861)" is
   not a property of the term.** The number is the correlation of the term's contribution *summed
   over the 6 s horizon of a policy that drives through the lead*, and that sum largely counts the
   steps before contact: it correlates +0.920 with the TTC. Divided by the time to contact the
   correlation with the share who intervene is +0.119, and the required deceleration itself, at the
   released constants, correlates **+0.633** with the share, and +0.74 to +0.94 within every closing
   speed. With the released step form of the term, the total orders the cells the human way within
   every closing speed (+0.76 to +0.94); the negative values come with the project's own ramp form,
   which every card of the arc ran with and labeled "released".
2. **Three supporting cards are invalid as run.** In card RE.4 the ego was off the road in all 378
   cells (world lateral position about 6 m against a lane centre of 0; 450,000 nats of constant).
   Card RE.1's "the released planner steers in 36 of 36 cells and never brakes" reads only the
   first step of each plan, which the pedal constraint clamps to -0.1 m/s² for any plan. Card
   JJ.2b's planner rows share both defects, so "the direction is repairable with steering (19 of
   24)" is void.
3. **What does stand** is narrower and still useful: card JJ.2's DROP (Delta G does not order the
   cut-in cells; 0.2515 with the sign free, against the gap's 0.1522), and the matched-TTC result
   (0 of 24 rows ordered the human way, under both forms of the term): the quantity's severity
   grows with closing speed while participants respond to proximity.
4. **The two "things that went the right way" both need restating.** The comfort margin "beats the
   gap" only at a_OV = -10, the setting that won an exploratory sweep; at Jonas's ruled primary of
   -6 it scores 0.2230 and does not. Card JJ.4's direction is robust (every driver fold positive;
   the video/track ratio is above 1 under every prior tried), but its magnitudes are not, and
   "clean" overstates it.
5. **Nothing from this arc should go to the authors as it stands**, in particular not "inverted",
   not "never brakes", and not the 0.5 s headway statement without its caveat (section 4).

The practical consequence: the arc's negative conclusion about Delta G on the cut-in survives; its
*explanation* does not, and the claim that "no parameter vector, no functional and no preference
structure repairs it" is weaker than the handover says, because one of its three legs was never
tested (RE.4; now partly retested as RE.4b) and another used a confounded grid (RE.2, section 3).

## 3 Process findings

* **Result strings typed into scripts.** Rule 1 asks that every quoted number come from a committed
  script with a tracked output. Three scripts write a result as literal text into their report
  (checks §7), and two of those literals are wrong or unverified. Several cards hard-code their
  *readings* as prose in the generator, written after a first run.
* **Pre-registration cannot be verified from git.** Every card of the arc lands script, output and
  worklog entry in one commit, so "written before the run" is unverifiable. The earlier arcs used a
  separate "pre-stated" commit (EX.1, EX.2, PC.1); that practice should be the rule. The cards run
  tonight follow it.
* **"Released" was used for the project's staging.** Every rollout card ran with
  `counterfactual_residual_severity=True` and `lane_entry_continuous=True`, which are the project's
  own flags (the implementer's brief directed it). The arc's statements about "the released model"
  are statements about that variant. This matters before anything is raised with the authors.
* **The framing "the program itself puts the comfort zone in strand 1" is the project's reading.**
  Its only source is our own `notes/01_paper_summaries.md`; "comfort zone" occurs in neither paper,
  and the poster speaks of "three main aspects". The literature claims themselves check out
  (Table 2's tolerances, "set by hand", the data-availability statement, the code-availability
  statement). One detail the build note omitted is directly useful: strand 1 defines conflict as
  the longitudinal distance falling below **"a safe distance (2 m)"**, which is a standoff.
* **The register.** S13.Q1 to Q4 are cited in the handover and do not exist in the worklog; the
  handover's counts are off by one; WP.Q3 (the alpha setting behind RE.1 part C) is filed as minor.
* **The naming rule was broken twice** (worklog, handover §7a): the colleague was named. Both are

## 5 The three cards run tonight

**S1.5, the comfort threshold (Jonas's idea).** Writing it down showed three things before any
score: the "different maximum decel" is the fitted *level* of the threshold model, so it is read,
not swept; on this study the assumed lead braking and the standoff are the same parameter, because
the lead's speed is 24.99 m/s in every cell; and the level can be read as a comfortable deceleration
only under a counterfactual a driver would hold. Result, on the pre-stated rules: **no arm is a
comfort-zone candidate.** The released counterfactual with a 2 m standoff scores 0.2147 with a
median level of 11.8 m/s². Fitted (nested, so no test cell informs the choice), it reaches
**0.1132, level with looming's 0.1137, and with card G.1's gate frozen 0.1020 / 0.0317 against the
gated looming rule's 0.1027 / 0.0319**, but at a 40 m standoff (the grid's edge, in every fold) and
a level of 31.5 m/s². A 40 m standoff cancels the lead's stopping credit almost exactly, so what
the data choose is "stop where the lead is NOW, half a second from now": a proximity rule written
in deceleration units, not a braking judgment. The lead-holds-speed counterfactual, whose levels
are plausible decelerations (3.4 to 6 m/s² at a 10 m standoff), scores 0.146 to 0.162 and 0.1539
nested: not credited. **Where the quantity orders the cells its level is not a deceleration anyone
could produce, and where its level is comfortable it does not order the cells.**

**S1.6, admissibility (S14.Q2).** Jonas asked what "the minimum is over admissible policies only"
implies. Four things (`out/s16_admissibility.md` §0): Delta G stops being a difference and becomes
a *price*, the comfort cost of the mildest admissible deceleration, so the strand-1 line and the
comfort-margin line are one line; it contains a gate, which is a tolerance alpha on the probability
of conflict; it has a ceiling, the cells where nothing is admissible; and it reads the fan's tails,
which is where the flaw in JJ.1's fan was found. Result: **not credited** (0.3107; rule (d) holds).
The price is the kinematic required deceleration against a lead that holds its speed, which is
anti-ordered within matched-TTC rows (2 of 24). What did emerge is the gate: at alpha 0.10 to 0.20
`continue` is admissible in 90 of 90 pre-onset cells and in 0 to 2 of 288 post-onset cells, with no
gate term. It does not show in the pre-onset score (0.0954 at best) because the axis above it is at
chance.

**RE.4b, card RE.4 with the ego on the road.** Each cell's preference is staged with the lane centre
at the ego's own recorded position, by wrapping the two functions RE.4 calls; not a line of RE.4 is
edited. Epsilon now runs 1,452 to 388,477 nats (it was 450,018 to 660,185). **Not credited**: the
three agent-side functionals score 0.3202, 0.3215 and 0.3202 at the sign their definition implies
(a constant prediction again, as in card JJ.2) and 0.3007, 0.2756 and 0.3019 with the sign
reversed; epsilon correlates -0.214 with the share and the epistemic term -0.462. So for these
three functionals card RE.4's conclusion now rests on a valid construction. Its two planner-side
functionals remain untested (query RE4B.Q1).
