# The authors' edition: what should change since the 2026-09-03 revision

*Review of 2026-09-28, at Jonas's request ("check if there are any changes/updates we should make to
the author handbook"). The edition (`aif_driver_model_handbook.md`) has been sent to the authors;
nothing in it is changed by this note. Each item gives the place, the reason, and proposed text in
the edition's own style ([Study] for our analyses, [Opinion] for readings, no comfort-zone framing,
no internal file paths). Every number is from a tracked output named in the notes after each item.*

## Recommendation in one paragraph

Five passages are overtaken by what we have learned since 3 September. Three of them concern the
released model's own behavior, and the authors would want to know about them. (1) The model brakes
during sustained following, now observed rather than extrapolated. (2) Its response-time relation
holds in direction on real motorway data. (3) Its calibrated following preference and its inverse-tau
term are rarely active in real traffic. One passage gains the naturalistic check it said was missing
(4, looming against inverse tau on real cut-ins), and one [Opinion] should be softened (5, the road
departures). **I recommend a short dated addendum rather than a reissue.** Send it only after Julian
has answered the draft email (`correspondence/2026-09-25_draft_email_julian.md`), whose point 3 asks
about item 1. If he knows a setting or a run-in that removes the braking, item 1 should say so. The
edition's README already says it is revised "only when a review of the published model warrants it";
items 1 to 3 do.

## 1 Chapter 5, "Normal driving: the quiet regime": the drift is now observed (priority: high)

**Now.** "Extrapolated, the accumulator would re-plan on its own after 2–7 s of uneventful following at
gaps of 2 s or less; the published simulations do not show this because they start 0.8 s before the
lead brakes. … The drift is a real property with small near-conflict consequences; designs with long
benign run-ins are where it would bite [Study]."

**Why.** We have since run sustained following, and the model re-plans and brakes. Behind a lead at
constant speed (15 m/s, the authors' own scripted lead as well as a replay), the released configuration
starts braking after 3.2 s at a 1.5 s headway and after 4.6 s at 2.0 s. It does so with gaze choice on
or off, and 1 to 3 of 4 repeats brake to a standstill. With perception noise × 100 it follows steadily.
Behind real leads replayed from the highD dataset, in 18 five-second episodes in which the lead's
acceleration never exceeds 0.5 m/s² in magnitude, it brakes by at least 1 m/s² in 56% of runs. The
braking usually starts 2.5–5 s in and often reaches −6 to −7 m/s². The human drivers in the same
episodes never did (their minimum −0.63 m/s²). The episodes were staged with the authors' own
calibration (`find_parameters`), which returned the same values for all 18 (desired-speed offset 0,
assumed lead braking −8 m/s²). The cause is not established.

**Proposed text** (replacing the two sentences above; keep the 23-scenario surrogate result):

> *Revised 2026-09-28.* In sustained following the drift does trigger re-plans, and the re-plans brake.
> Behind a lead holding a constant speed, the released configuration began braking after 3.2 s at a
> 1.5 s headway and after 4.6 s at 2.0 s, with or without gaze choice and with the scenario's own
> scripted lead; some repeats braked to a standstill; with perception noise raised a hundredfold it
> followed steadily. Behind real leads replayed from the highD dataset, in 18 five-second episodes in
> which nothing happened, it braked by at least 1 m/s² in 56% of runs, typically 2.5–5 s in and often
> hard, where the drivers of the same episodes did not brake at all [Study]. The published scenarios
> never exercise this regime, and nothing in them is wrong because of it; it matters for any use of
> the model with a run-in longer than a few seconds. What makes braking win at the first re-plan, and
> why noisier perception suppresses it, we have not established [Opinion].

*Sources: GZ.1 (`replication/causation/gz1/`), GZ.2 (`replication/causation/gz2/`), NM.1 and NM.1b
(`replication/czb/out/nm1_replayed_following.md`, `nm1b_figure.md`).*

## 2 Chapter 5, "What this means for using the model": the response-time relation on real data (priority: high, positive)

**Why.** The paper's central timing validation is the growth of response time with the initial time
gap. On 1,033 real highD lead-deceleration events the relation has the right direction and steepens
with the lead's severity. The level differs by a factor of about three, for reasons that are mostly the
data's (few emergencies; a slow ramp of real lead braking).

**Proposed text** (a new bullet after "Response time is not a parameter you set"):

> - *Added 2026-09-28.* **The response-time relation holds in direction on real motorway data.** On
>   1,033 lead-deceleration events in the highD dataset, the log of the follower's response time rises
>   with the log of the initial time gap, and more steeply the harder the lead brakes: slopes +0.23,
>   +0.40 and +0.45 for mild, moderate and hard lead decelerations, against +0.59 in the deposited runs.
>   Real responses are about three times slower (median 2.2–3.1 s at 1–2 s gaps against 0.8 s). Most of
>   that is the data: few motorway events are emergencies (32 leads braked at 3 m/s² or more), and real
>   lead braking takes about 2 s to build up where the scenario's takes a tenth of that. With so few
>   hard events this is descriptive, not a test [Study].

*Source: NM.3 (`replication/czb/out/nm3_response_time.md`); detector timing validated in NC.0b-lat.*

## 3 Chapter 7, the preference terms: how often they act in real traffic (priority: medium)

**Why.** Two measurements on real traffic describe the preference terms' operating range. They are
properties of the terms, not criticisms: a collision-avoidance preference is expected to be silent in
ordinary driving.

**Proposed text** (at the end of Part A, or in the chapter's level-2 notes after the inverse-tau
sentence):

> *Added 2026-09-28.* **How often the terms act on real traffic.** Evaluated on about a million
> car-following samples from the highD dataset, the calibrated braking-margin term is violated in 1.7%
> of steady following and the inverse-tau term in 0.05%. The headway at which the margin starts to
> cost, at equal speeds, lies at or below the fifth percentile of real headways in four of five speed
> bands (0.70 s at 40–70 km/h down to 0.05 s above 130 km/h, against real fifth percentiles of
> 0.54–0.67 s). On 2,208 real closing cut-ins at the moment the cutting-in car enters the lane, 99.5%
> have a time to collision of 5 s or more, where the one-sided inverse-tau term sits at its floor and
> costs nothing. Real followers nevertheless brake in some of those cases, mostly gently, many at 5–20 s. Both terms
> are shaped for avoiding collisions, which is what the model is for, and leave most of ordinary
> driving to the gentler terms [Study].

*Sources: NM.2 (`replication/czb/out/nm2_following_preference.md`), NC.5-tau (worklog 2026-09-24),
NC.3.*

## 4 Chapter 3, "The looming channel, tested against human judgments": the naturalistic check (priority: medium)

**Now.** The passage ends: "Whether the reversal is the paradigm …, the task … or the scenario … is
open; naturalistic onsets would settle it [Opinion]."

**Why.** We now have naturalistic onsets. On real cut-ins, followers' braking is predicted slightly
*better* by inverse TTC than by looming, which agrees with Xue et al. for executed braking. That favors
the task or paradigm explanation, and it is good news for the released model's inverse-tau term.

**Proposed text** (replacing the last sentence):

> … is open. *Revised 2026-09-28.* Naturalistic onsets point to the task or the paradigm: on 2,208
> real closing cut-ins in the highD dataset, whether the follower brakes within 3 s is predicted
> slightly better by inverse time to collision than by the expansion rate (area under the ROC curve
> 0.739 against 0.713; difference 0.026, 95% interval 0.006 to 0.043), at every braking threshold we
> tried and at short and long gaps alike. So video judgments follow the expansion rate, while executed
> braking in real traffic follows inverse tau, as Xue et al. found in the simulator. The model's
> perceptual front end and its inverse-tau preference may each be right for its own job [Opinion].

*Sources: NC.3 and NC.3c (`replication/czb/out/nc3_highd_cutins.md`; worklog 2026-09-24).*

**Optional one-line caveat on the same passage** (no numbers change): "The clips were watched on the
raters' own monitors, which minify the rendered field of view; the ranking of the candidate
quantities is unaffected by a constant minification, absolute looming levels would be [Study]."
Include only if later text quotes absolute looming levels; the present passage does not.

## 5 Chapter 5, the 25 m/s road departures: soften the reading (priority: low, courtesy)

**Now.** "… so we read these departures as a property of the lane-change control at speed rather than
as a deliberate trade-off [Opinion]; either way, analyses at 25 m/s should track road departure as its
own outcome class."

**Why.** The authors' reply of 2026-09-11 pointed to the road departures as one place where our
reading was one-sided. The edition should keep the observation and the practical advice without taking
a side it cannot support. (The reply is private; nothing from it should be quoted.)

**Proposed text:**

> … At a 3.5 s gap moderate braking would suffice. Whether these departures are best read as a
> property of the lane-change control at speed or as a trade-off the preference landscape makes on
> purpose is a question of reading on which we would defer to the authors [Opinion]; either way,
> analyses at 25 m/s should track road departure as its own outcome class.

## What should NOT go into the authors' edition

- **The comfort-zone work** (the gated looming measurement model, its free-energy reading, the two
  boundaries, the display transform). By the edition's design this is the project's own agenda.
- **The horizon-sum observation** (summing a one-sided preference over a policy that drives through
  the lead counts the steps before contact). It concerns *our* use of the expected free energy for
  comfort judgments. It is in the note to JJ, which is marked "do not send as it stands".
- **Anything from the 09-18 to 09-22 arc** that the review withdrew (for example "p_safe is inverted").
- **The planner rerun on the video cells** (P.1: brakes and steers from the first step in nearly every
  cell). It is the same phenomenon as item 1, in a staging of our own (110–130 km/h cut-in clips far
  outside the scenarios' range). Item 1 says it better.
