## 1 Where things stand, in five sentences

Card JJ.1's rollout formulation was built, run on all three scenarios, and **dropped** — its axis
scores chance. Five further cards then established that this is not a defect of that construction:
no parameter vector of the released preference orders the cells (RE.2, 648 swept), no functional of
its expected free energy does (RE.4, five tried), and neither does a change of preference structure
to strand 1's (S1.4). The diagnosis that survives all of them is that **the released
braking-margin term is inverted against the human response** (ρ = −0.861, RE.2; confirmed by
deletion in S1.4), and that the project had been using a *collision-avoidance* model to measure a
*comfort zone*, which the program itself puts in a different strand. Two things went the right way
and are the live leads: the **comfort margin** — the released model's own `required_deceleration`,
read without the threshold and the severity — beats the best design scalar (S1.1, 0.1409 against
0.1522), and **card JJ.4's observation-model result** stands untouched by any of this. Nothing is
pushed; the tree is clean; the suite is green at 31/33/40/96/62/20/28/27/16/30/33/35.

## 3 The through-line, because five cards say one thing

**The released model is a collision-avoidance model and the comfort zone is a different regime.**
The program's own IWAI 2024 poster lays out three strands; the comfort zone belongs to strand 1
(Engström et al. 2024), and this project took strand 2's model (Schumann et al.). Our own
`notes/01_paper_summaries.md` has said so since 2026-08-17 and nothing acted on it until now.

Consequences, each measured:

1. **On these stimuli ε is collision + safety and nothing else** — the speed, acceleration,
   steering and lane factors are zero to machine precision (−1.2e-14 at all 378 cells), because the
   stimulus ego holds its desired speed in its lane and never acts.
2. **The braking-margin term is inverted** against the response (ρ −0.861), and it is the term
   `preferences.py` itself calls "the comfort-zone term". Strand 1 has no such term at all.
3. **No constant, no functional and no preference structure repairs it** (RE.2, RE.4, S1.4).
4. **But the underlying quantity is fine.** `required_deceleration` read without the threshold and
   the severity beats the gap (S1.1). The material was there; the assembly is what fails.

## 6 What I propose next, in order

**S1.5 — the comfort threshold. This is Jonas's own idea and I think it is the best thing in the
arc.** He wrote: *"if it was fitted with a different maximum decel and a position a bit further
out, it could be CZB, or?"* He is right, and the arithmetic says why. At zero relative speed with
the released constants, `required_deceleration` is **not** flat — it runs −7.55, −6.05, −5.05,
−4.33, −3.79, −3.37, −3.04 m/s² across headways 0.5 to 3.5 s at 110 km/h. RE.1 found ε flat at
23 nats there only because the *indicator* at a_max = 8 never fires in that range. So:

- threshold the same quantity at a **comfortable** deceleration instead of the physical maximum,
  and it becomes a graded boundary that sits inside the range drivers actually use;
- add a **standoff margin** ("a position a bit further out") and the whole curve shifts, which is a
  second, independent driver parameter;
- and both are exactly the two parameters the reformulation note proposed fitting **per driver**.

The card: score the margin `a_req + a_comfort` on card JJ.2's cells, folds and metric, sweeping the
comfort threshold and the standoff, with the primary fixed **in advance** by motivation. It builds
on S1.1's machinery, needs no new estimator, and is hours. **Its risk, stated now:** S1.1 already
showed the underlying quantity beats the gap, so part of this result is banked; what is new is
whether a *threshold* form gives the gate that S1.1 conspicuously did not (its pre-onset score was
0.4745 against card G.1's 0.0319).

**S1.6 — admissibility (S14.Q2, authorized).** Implement strand 1's absolute conflict preference by
excluding colliding policies from the menu rather than costing them. This changes ΔG's definition —
the minimum is then over admissible policies only — and S1.4's sweep showed the finite-constant
version is consequential (−300/−1000/−3000 give 0.3244/0.3221/0.2864). Half a day.

**Ask Engström for the strand-1 code (S12.Q1)** before building it. The Frontiers data-availability
statement points at him and Jonas has already spoken to him; the verbatim wording for the email is
in §7.

**Then the strand-1 build ladder** in `docs/strand1_build_note.md` §5 — **with its S1.4 withdrawn**,

## 7 The three things he asked that are not yet answered in a file

**(a) Does the 0.5 s headway result make sense?** Yes, and it is not a bug in our code. The released
counterfactual credits the **lead** with its own stopping distance (`x_ν − v_ν²/2a_test`), so at
equal speeds the ego only has to stop short of where the lead *ends up*. At a 0.5 s headway at
110 km/h that gives a_req = −7.55 m/s², just inside the a_max = 8 threshold, so the term does not
fire. It is a coherent consequence of the model's own assumptions and it is a defect only for
comfort, not for collision avoidance. **Worth raising with Johan**, because it is the cleanest
one-line statement of why the term is a dread boundary.

**(b) S14.Q1, which he said he did not understand.** Rephrased: card S1.4's arm 3 is strand 1's wide
tolerances **plus** the braking-margin term deleted **but** the released *graded* collision cost
kept. It is the best of the four arms on every measure. The trouble is that it is **not a model
anyone has proposed** — strand 1 has a *flat* conflict preference, the Nature model *has* p_safe —
so arm 3 is a chimera assembled by deleting one term from one model and keeping a term from the
other. The question is whether that combination is worth developing as a construction in its own
right, or whether its advantage is an artefact of deleting a term the rest of the model was
calibrated around. **It needs his judgment, not more computation.**

**(c) The verbatim statement for his email to Engström.** Frontiers, *Resolving uncertainty on the
fly*, Front. Neurorobot. 18:1341750, "Data availability statement":

> "The original contributions presented in the study are included in the article/supplementary
> material, further inquiries can be directed to the corresponding author."

Useful contrast to include: the Nature paper's own "Code availability" section releases the code at
`github.com/tud-hri/Active-Inference-Collision-Avoidance` (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20049511) under a
non-commercial licence permitting research, teaching and scientific publication. So the group does
release; the Frontiers statement simply points at him.

## 8 What a fresh session should not redo

- Do not re-test ΔG, min G, ε or the epistemic term as axes on the cut-in. RE.2 and RE.4 closed
  that: no constant and no functional works, and the numbers are on file.
- Do not build a car-following model to supply the ego's steady state. Jonas's objection of
  2026-09-22 is correct and is recorded in the worklog: the stimulus already supplies the steady
  state, and importing IDM or a required-decel controller would mean measuring that model instead
  of the boundary. `docs/strand1_build_note.md` §5's S1.4 is withdrawn for this reason.
- Do not treat strand 1's published constants as a reference. The paper says they "were set by hand
  and no systematic model optimization was performed"; σ_v and σ_a are driver parameters in this
  project's reformulation and should be initialised from them, never fixed at them (S13.Q2).
- Do not quote card S1.1's 0.1409 as a confirmed result. Part of that card was exploratory and its
  constant won a sweep; S1.2's attempt to confirm it was inconclusive because study 1's cut-in is
  one-dimensional, and **the second cut-in study is the only two-dimensional design this project
  has**.
