## 2 The findings a successor must not re-derive

**GZ2.Q2** (`replication/causation/gz2/gz2_following_braking.md`, eleven conditions). In steady following
at 15 m/s and 1.5 s headway the released model re-plans by itself at step 13 and brakes, fed entirely by
the collision-and-safety term (0.0764 evidence per step). What stops it:

| condition | what was scaled ×100 | evidence per step | re-plans |
|---|---|---|---|
| B0 released | nothing | 0.0764 | step 13–14 |
| C3 | all seven state channels | 0.0023 | never |
| D1 | longitudinal: x, v, a | 0.0748 | step 13–14 |
| D2 | lateral and heading: y, θ, δ, ω | 0.0024 | never |
| D3 | D2's scales in the planner's decoder only | 0.0764 | step 13–14 |

The lateral and heading channels carry the effect, through the particle filter's belief update and not
through the observations the planner samples. The scales are the model's *assumed* observation noise, not
the world's (the environment uses them ×0.001). The hypothesis that a loose lateral belief exempts the
imagined lead from the safety checks was refuted (part E: the in-path share is 1.000 in both). The step
from a loosened lateral belief to fewer imagined collisions is open (GZ2.Q3, minor).

**Card PC.1** (`replication/czb/out/pc1_projected_conflict.md`; design note §6b). The construction: the
other road user's positions over a horizon, projected at constant velocity, against the ego's path
corridor, with the ego's path read either from its current motion (K) or its recorded future (P); gate
Φ((m − c)/s) in G.1's form, held once open. Results, identical in both runs apart from one check:

| test | result |
|---|---|
| cut-in, G.1's criteria | credited by 0.0001: post-onset 0.1126 against the bound 0.1127, CP1 0.0356; the gate is a step (0.952 post-onset, 0.000 at CP1) because a projected incursion is clamped at zero clearance |
| left turn, consistency | K closed in all 18 cells at every horizon; P open in 2, 6, 9, 14 of 18 at 3, 4, 5, 6 s; not consistent at any horizon |
| left turn, cost against distance alone (0.0558) | +0.232 at 3 s, +0.024 at 6 s |
| overtake | open from the first evaluated moment in all 15 cells under every reading |
| adoption | none |

The reading on file, marked opinion: anticipated conflict starts the response in every scenario, but each
scenario has its own anticipation horizon (about three seconds on the cut-in, five to seven on the left
turn, none needed on the overtake). One fixed-horizon construction is not the scenario-agnostic onset.

**Two corrections to the construction were made before any run**, found by the property tests: the
corridor is the ego's path over 20 s while only the other's positions are limited to the horizon, and the
clearance has one ordering by role. **One was made after the first run**: the planned path's continuation
past a trace's end used the last two jittered samples; the cut-in's |cK − cP| check fell from 1.619 m to
0.598 m after the fix. All three are dated in the note, the module and the script.

## 5 What this session may have got wrong

- PC.1's test 1 passed by 0.0001. A credit that narrow is a coin toss; the step-shaped gate is the real
  finding, and PC1.Q5 (a signed clearance) would restore G.1's grading.
- The left-turn "anticipation horizon of five to seven seconds" is read off where the planned-path gate
  opens, not measured as a horizon; it is the oncoming car's arrival time at the crossing, which is the
  design's PET structure. Whether participants anticipate that far, or simply respond to distance from the
  outset, PC.1 cannot separate.

- `handover.md` §7 still says "no `gh`". The machine now has `gh` logged in as Jonas; the repo-local token

## 9 The findings a successor must not re-derive

**Card EX.1** (`out/ex1_exposure_split.md`). Repeated exposure does not teach a pre-onset anticipation.
Second cut-in study, 144 participants, every clip shown twice: pre-onset responding unchanged on the second
showing (0.023 → 0.024, +0.001 [−0.011, +0.013]); post-onset +0.027 [+0.010, +0.045]. Study-1 left turn,
each cell four times over two sessions: responding falls about 9 points in every distance band, mostly at
the session boundary; the distance rule's boundary 87.4 → 77.4 m (ratio 0.885 [0.827, 0.958]), sharpness
unchanged. Neither effect is reliable per driver (split-half 0.35). Correction on record: the +0.027 in
`out/cutin2_scope.md` was post-onset only. Consequence for PC.1: the left turn's long anticipation horizon
is largest at first exposure, so it is not learned from repetition.

**Card EX.2** (`out/ex2_first_exposure_levels.md`). All showings kept; one shared session term on the level;
reported at first exposure. Study-1 cut-in counted with no model: session 2 − session 1 +0.021
[−0.012, +0.056], not shown negligible. Pooled refits reproduce stage 1 exactly and TT.1 to 0.004 s; a
synthetic session shift recovers (+0.300 → +0.275, SE 0.092). Session term: cut-in beta −0.145
[−0.237, −0.052] log rad/s, first-exposure median 0.0341 rad/s (pooled 0.0317; trigger +0.136 s later);
left turn beta +0.476 [+0.290, +0.663] s, PET_50 2.42 s at first exposure (pooled 2.18 s). **First
exposure is the primary setting in both scenarios** (pre-stated Rule A). The per-driver trait is unchanged
(+0.642 against +0.647). Arithmetic on committed numbers, not a refit: the video-to-track offset on the
left turn shrinks from −0.27 s to about −0.03 s (track 2.45 s); neither difference is significant, and the
track drivers are four times sharper either way.

**The naturalistic plan** (`docs/naturalistic_data_plan.md`, + docx, pdf). highD and inD applications sent
